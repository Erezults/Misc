Technology Generations

Gen 1 (Legacy): UV, analog, high FAR. AN/AAR-47

Gen 2 (Current): UV digital, HFI, near-zero FAR. CMWS, MILDS Block 2, AAR-54, MAW-300

Gen 3 (Advanced): MWIR cooled, AI/ML, multi-function. 2CAWS, PAWS-2 HR, Elix-IR

Gen 4 (Emerging 2027+): High-res uncooled MWIR, multi-sensor fusion, autonomous classification >95%, edge AI. US Army ITDS (ATHENA)

Key Insight

Market migrating UV → MWIR in 2024-2028. Two-color MWIR + ML achieves <1 false alarm/flight hour vs. 2-10/hr for single-band. This is a hard architectural requirement for any new entrant.

Rafael cannot achieve market-competitive FAR without two-color/multi-spectral + ML. Design from start for MWIR + AI architecture.

Processing Architecture Trends

Edge AI Processing

FPGA + NPU co-processing in single chip (Xilinx Versal, Intel Agilex). Real-time inference at <15W. Eliminates separate processing box.

Fiber Optic Backbone

BAE 2CAWS uses fiber optic A-kit replacing copper harnesses. Reduces weight, eliminates EMI coupling on electrically noisy rotorcraft.

System-in-Package (SiP)

Detector ROIC + signal conditioning + digital output in single package. Reduces size and EMI.

Rafael's ML Opportunity

  • Sky Spotter already incorporates AI-based image processing for multi-target detection in C-UAS domain - directly applicable heritage
  • Challenge: adapt models from slow-moving drones to high-velocity missiles
  • Key gap: Labeled missile plume signature training dataset is the most defensible IP moat - harder to acquire than algorithms
  • Needs IDF/IAF cooperation or international test range access

Top 3 Pain Points (5-Whys Analysis)

Pain #1: High False Alarm Rate

Root Cause: Legacy UV sensors with rule-based algorithms cannot discriminate in modern complex environments.

Evidence: Apache alarm anomalies documented; MITRE/FAA alarm complacency research

Solution: MWIR dual-band + embedded ML classification (validated by 2CAWS, PAWS-2). Design native MWIR + AI architecture.

Pain #2: Integration Complexity

Root Cause: Absence of open/modular architecture standards for MWS. Proprietary interfaces (BAE, Elbit) lock customers into full-suite integration.

Impact: 12-36 months NRE per platform, uneconomical for smaller fleets.

Solution: Open-architecture MWS with standardized interfaces (MIL-STD-1553, Ethernet/ARINC 664, FACE-compliant software).

Pain #3: SWaP Limitations

Root Cause: Heritage designs sized for large helicopters. Cooled MWIR required large dewar assemblies. Requirements written by/for large platform operators.

Impact: Light helicopters, UAVs, patrol boats, light vehicles unprotected.

Solution: Uncooled/TE-cooled MWIR + compact SoC processors. Target <800g, <10W per head for UAV/light platforms.

Lifecycle Cost Example

100-aircraft fleet, 20-year MWS program:

  • Acquisition: ~$40M
  • O&S (spares, maintenance, SW updates): ~$90-100M+
  • Total ownership: ~$130-140M

Key O&S drivers:

  • Cooled detector maintenance ($15-30K/event) - uncooled eliminates
  • Software currency/threat library updates
  • Diminishing manufacturing sources (DMS) for legacy components
  • False alarm-driven maintenance (indirect cost multiplier)

Certification Requirements

MIL-HDBK-516, MIL-STD-810, DO-160 (civil carry). 3-5 years per jurisdiction for new MWS. Longest lead item in market entry plan.

Unmet Needs & Market Gaps

Gap 1: MALE UAV MWS

CRITICAL - Zero Incumbent

No qualified SWaP-optimized EO MWS for MALE drones. Addressable fleet: 1,000+ aircraft globally (MQ-9, Heron TP, TB2, Hermes 900). Asset values $15-30M. First-mover advantage available.

Requirement: <800g/head, <10W, uncooled/TE-cooled, MAVLink + STANAG 4586

Gap 2: Affordable Ground Vehicle HFI

UNDERSERVED

Standalone affordable (<$50K) MWIR + acoustic fusion HFI for APCs/tactical vehicles. AN/AAR-47 HFI is US-only. Vehicle fleets measured in thousands. Ukraine conflict accelerated demand globally.

Requirement: 360 deg, <2 kg, <20W, CAN bus, <$50-100K

Gap 3: Naval Surface EO MWS

NICHE - HIGH VALUE

No widely deployed dedicated EO MWS for surface combatants. Radar-centric defense is vulnerable to passive IR-homing threats. Smaller navies (SE Asia, Middle East, S. America) with corvettes/OPVs have no viable solution.

Requirement: 4π coverage, <1s reaction, MIL-S-901, CMS integration

Gap 4: ITAR-Free Open Architecture MWS

POLICY-DRIVEN

India, Turkey, Gulf states, SE Asia frustrated with US ITAR constraints on CMWS/LAIRCM. Need trusted non-US supplier enabling mixed-origin fleet (Mi-17, Supercougar, CN-235) equipment without FMS complications.

Market positioning gap, not technology gap. Elbit proves Israeli ITAR-free wins.

Gap 5: AI-Native Full-Spectrum HFI

TECH GAP CLOSING

Convergence of MWIR + AI enabling simultaneous MANPADS + RPG + ATGM + small arms detection in single sensor head. Technically achievable now (Elbit claims) but not universally available at competitive SWaP/cost for non-US customers.

Gap 6: Scalable Multi-Platform Family

STRUCTURAL

Solutions designed for Apache don't fit UAVs; naval solutions don't adapt to vehicles. No vendor offers modular family sharing common sensor head (3 size variants), processing unit, and SW across airborne/naval/ground. Would reduce qualification cost and give major commercial leverage.

Threats & Headwinds

Competitive Barriers

Incumbent Lock-In

BAE CMWS and Northrop LAIRCM benefit from US Army logistics, training, and maintenance infrastructure creating high switching costs. Installed base of 15,000-20,000+ BAE sensors and 7,000+ Hensoldt sensors in Europe.

Sustainment contracts provide recurring revenue that funds next-gen development.

Certification Barriers

Airworthiness certification (MIL-HDBK-516, MIL-STD-810, DO-160): 3-5 years per jurisdiction for a new MWS. Missing certification windows can delay revenue by years. This is the longest lead item.

US Market Closed

DoD requirements for domestic sourcing effectively lock Rafael out of US Army/USAF/USN market. Buy American Act, ITAR controls, and established FMS programs favor BAE and Northrop.

Market & Operational Risks

Budget Risks

Defense budget cycles are political. Economic downturns or policy shifts could slow procurement. European spending surge is partially driven by Ukraine crisis - resolution could reduce urgency.

Export Control Complexity

Israeli defense exports controlled by SIBAT. US-origin components trigger ITAR re-export requirements. MWIR FPAs, ML software, and DIRCM cueing interfaces are heavily controlled. Export approvals add 12-24 months to delivery.

Competitive Timing Risk

BAE 2CAWS (400 units, 2024) and Elbit PAWS-2 HR (2025) have first-mover advantage. Rafael entering 5 years after means competing against mature, fielded systems with established logistics chains. No new entrants have broken in 2023-2026.

Internal Risks

Rafael's Sky Shield and X-Guard serve adjacent missions - internal cannibalization risk must be managed. Brand recognition: Rafael is not currently thought of as an MWS vendor - marketing investment required.

Top Strategic Recommendations

Common sensor head (3 sizes: UAV/mini, helicopter, naval/ground), common processing, common SW. Platform-agnostic, DIRCM-agnostic. Open architecture as differentiator vs. BAE/Elbit proprietary stacks.

No incumbent. 1,000+ addressable aircraft. Sub-800g HOT/uncooled sensor from Rafael/SCD technology. Launch on Heron TP as internal customer. First-mover advantage.

4. Design ITAR-Free from Day One

Maximize Israeli-developed components. No US-origin detectors/FPGAs/processors. Enables India, Gulf, SE Asia, Eastern Europe markets. Mirrors Elbit's proven export strategy.

5. Pursue Strategic Partnerships

European JV: Qualify for EDF funding, EU-origin certification. India offset: ToT to HAL/BEL for 156 LCH + Mi-17 fleet. DIRCM partner: Northrop CIRCM (US access) or Leonardo Miysis (EU).

Key Design Principles

  • AI-first architecture: ML threat discrimination baked into sensor head, not added later
  • Two-color MWIR: Hard requirement for <1 FAR/flight hour
  • Open interfaces: MIL-STD-1553, Ethernet, FACE, STANAG 4670 by default
  • Software-defined threat library: OTA-capable update architecture from day one
  • FAR as primary KPI: Design goal <1 nuisance alarm/10 flight hours in worst-case clutter
  • Uncooled/TE-cooled option: Eliminates dominant O&S cost driver (cooler maintenance)
